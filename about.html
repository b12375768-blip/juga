About

Copyright is not supposed to be this confusing

Creator Rights is an independent educational site for people who publish videos, streams, podcasts and social clips. We explain the rules that decide whether you can use someone else's music or footage — and exactly what to do when you get it wrong.

Why this site exists

Most creator copyright information is written for lawyers, or for people trying to sell something to creators. Neither is useful when a video you published yesterday has a claim on it and you do not know whether it is serious.

The gap is obvious. New creators routinely lose monetisation — and sometimes whole channels — to mistakes that take twenty minutes of reading to avoid: music borrowed from another creator's video, a clip that needed two permissions instead of one, a fair use argument that does not hold up because the purpose was entertainment all along.

So we wrote the guides we wanted when we started publishing. Practical, specific, and honest about where the law is genuinely uncertain.

Who it is for

  • Video creators on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and Twitch who use music, TV, film or stock footage.
  • Podcasters playing clips and music as illustrative examples.
  • Editors and freelancers licensing media on behalf of clients.
  • Small businesses and agencies running social accounts and ad campaigns.
  • Anyone who has ever received a copyright claim and did not know what to do next.

If you are a company employing in-house counsel, you need a lawyer rather than this site. That is not a criticism — this site exists because most creators do not have one.

How we work

Four editorial rules

Everything on this site follows these, which is why you can reasonably trust it as a starting point.

🎯

Specific, not vibes

Every guide names the actual situation: which platforms, which durations, which licence clauses, which deadlines. No "be careful with copyright".

⚖️

Honest about uncertainty

Where the law is genuinely unsettled or fact-specific, we say so instead of inventing a confident rule you can get hurt relying on.

🧾

Documentation first

We keep returning to the same advice: record the source, keep the licence, re-check when things change. It prevents more problems than any clever tactic.

🚫

No dark patterns

No email walls, no "download the ultimate pack", no affiliate links to music libraries, no ads dressed as advice.

What we deliberately don't do

  • We don't give legal advice. Everything here is general educational information. If a real dispute is on the line, talk to an attorney in your jurisdiction.
  • We don't sell or broker licences. We are not a music licensing agency and we take no commission on anything.
  • We don't ask for your email. Reading the guides requires nothing. The contact form is optional and used only to answer questions.

Corrections and updates

Platform policies change. Fair use case law evolves. Licences get rewritten. We review the guides on a regular cycle and update them when something material changes, and every page carries the date it was last reviewed.

If you find something inaccurate or out of date, we want to know. Send the detail through the contact form and we will check it against the source and fix it.
