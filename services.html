Part 1

Licence request emails

Short, specific, and easy to say yes to. Full guidance in Asking Artists for Permission.

1. Independent artist — music licence

The workhorse. Under 150 words, one clear ask.

Subject: Licence request — "[Track]" for a video

Hi [artist],

I'm a YouTube creator putting together a video about [topic], and I'd love to use "[Track Title]" as the background music.

Exactly what I'm asking for, so you can say yes or no without back-and-forth:

  • Track: [Track Title] ([album] version)
  • Use: ~[X] seconds as background music in a [length] video about [topic]
  • Platforms: YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, my website
  • Commercial: Yes — the video is monetised / sponsored
  • Term: perpetual, or [X] years
  • Credit: "[Artist] — [Track Title]" in the description and on screen
  • Offer: $[X], or prominent credit, or both

If you're open to it, could you reply with a short written confirmation covering the points above? Happy to sign a licence agreement if you have a standard one. And if it's not a fit, no problem at all.

2. Podcast host asking a guest

For interview clips, promotional material, and music played on the show.

Subject: Clip permission for the [date] episode

Hi [guest],

Thanks again for being on the show. I'd like to use a short excerpt from our conversation — approximately [X] seconds, covering [topic] — in:

  • The episode trailer, posted to [platforms]
  • A separate short clip on [platform], credited to you

Both are non-paid promotion for the episode, and I'll link to wherever you want people to find you. Could you reply confirming you're happy for me to do that?

If you'd prefer I don't use any clip, just say and I'll leave it out — no problem.

3. Rights holder — retroactive licence

When you've already published and now need permission. Lead with honesty.

Subject: Retroactive licence request (and removal offer)

Hi [name/rights team],

I own a channel, [channel link], around [X] subscribers. About [X] weeks ago I published a video, "[title]", which used approximately [X] seconds of "[track]" as background music. [Video URL].

I did not obtain a licence before publishing, and I am sorry — I had understood the track was available under a non-commercial licence, and I did not realise the video was monetised.

I am asking whether you would consider granting a retroactive licence for this use, for which I would be glad to pay your standard fee. If you would rather I did not, I will remove the track or take the video down today.

Thank you for your time.

4. Disputing an incorrect claim

When the material is not in fact yours. Factual, short, no accusations.

I dispute this Content ID claim on the basis that the material is my own original work and is not the work of the claimant.

What I used: an original track/footage titled "[title]", created and first published by me on [date] on [platform].
Evidence: original project files with timestamps, first publication, and [registration/distribution record].
Note on the match: the claim matches at [timestamp]; the matching section is [brief description], which differs from the claimant's work.

Please review and release the claim. I'm happy to provide further evidence.

5. Disputing with a licence you hold

The most common dispute. Attach the document, not a description of it.

I dispute this claim under a valid licence.

Work used: "[Track Title]" by [artist].
Licence: [purchased from library / granted directly by the artist] on [date].
Scope: cleared for [platforms], commercial/non-commercial, [term].
Evidence attached: [licence PDF / receipt / email thread].

Only this video uses the work. Please release the claim. Thank you.

6. Follow-up nudge

Send once, about a week later. Never more than twice.

Hi [artist],

Just bumping this in case it got buried. No rush at all — I know licensing stuff is a side quest. If it's easier, I'm happy to use a different track.

Thanks again,
[name]

Suggested folder structure

A single licences/ folder, subfolders per project or per year, filenames as YYYY-MM-DD_artist_track.pdf. Put a link to this log in your project notes so you find it in a panic.

Credit lines that work

Match the licence's required format exactly. Vague credits breach terms that otherwise cover you.

  • Stock music: ♫ [Track] by [Artist] — licensed via [Library] — [link]
  • Direct permission: ♫ [Track] by [Artist], used with permission
  • Public domain: [Title] (public domain) — source: [archive]
  • Creative Commons: [Title] by [Creator], used under CC BY 4.0 — [link]
  • Stock footage: Footage: [Clip Title] via [Library]
  • Fonts / graphics: [Font] by [Foundry] — free for personal and commercial use
Attribution guide

The 60-second pre-upload check

Run this before every single upload. Print it, or pin it next to your publish button.

  • Can I name the owner of every piece of music and footage in this video?
  • Can I produce the licence or permission right now?
  • Does it cover this platform and monetisation?
  • Is the excerpt length within what the licence allows?
  • Are the credits in the format the licence requires?
  • Is any borrowed material genuinely serving a fair use purpose?
  • Is my own music Content ID enabled?
  • Do I have a licensed fallback track ready if needed?
